## [1.0.0] - Unreleased

### Added
- [Let's Do Vinery](https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/mc-mods/lets-do-vinery)'s fermentation barrel is a station: take its lid off, pour the juice in and drop the ingredients in; once the lid is back on, everything in it ferments together in one wine's time (with the lid off it waits, and nothing is lost), and the wine waits in the barrel to be drawn at the bung into empty bottles. Juice and ingredients can always be taken back out.
- [Let's Do Farm & Charm](https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/mc-mods/lets-do-farm-charm)'s cooking pot is a station: throw the food into the water and stir with the pot's own stick. Nothing cooks unless it is stirred, the whole pot cooks as one batch, and wide steady rounds earn up to three stars that add servings. The soup waits in the pot and is filled into bowls dipped into it; the last bowl filled empties the pot at once. Food brought by hoppers, or cooked in the pot's own menu, cooks as Farm & Charm cooks it. The pot looks the same in the world as at the station, with its water and what floats in it, so nothing changes when you open it.
- Farm & Charm's stove is a station: the iron plate over its mouth swings open, the grate slides out and you lay on it what a bread, a cake or a roast is made of; coal goes into the firebox below, where you see it burn, and comes back out at any time, door open or shut. Shut the door and it bakes, the dough browning and rising; while the door stands open, the bake and the fire wait. The dish waits on the grate. The stove looks the same in the world as at the station, its door shut and its fire burning in the firebox, so nothing changes when you open it.
- Farm & Charm's roaster is a station: lay what the roast is made of into the pan, take the roaster by a handle (it lights up under the pointer) and swirl it as you would stir a pot. What lies in the pan lies there as thick pieces that brown as they roast (meat shrinks a little), slide about as the pan is swirled, knock against its walls and now and then turn over. The fat comes slowly: flat puddles under the food first, left behind where a piece slid, then a layer of oil over the floor that the pieces leave trails in; it seethes round the food, spits and steams, and the air over the hot pan shimmers. When it is done the pieces puff away and the roast lies glistening in the pan, and bowls take the servings. A hanging roaster works the same.
- [Let's Do HerbalBrews](https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/mc-mods/lets-do-herbal-brews)' tea kettles, iron and copper, are a station: the lid comes off, you pour water in from a bucket or a bottle and drop the leaves in; blaze powder at the kettle's foot makes it hotter. The kettle brews as it always does, and the tea waits in it: click the spout with a bottle and the kettle is picked up and poured. Hot, it steams from its spout and boils with HerbalBrews' own sound, and when a tea is done it whistles with a jet of steam, also for whoever passes by. Two small gauges show the water and the heat against what the drink needs.
- Vinery's apple press is a station: the basket's front swings open, you lay the apples into it and shut it, and press the cap of the screw. Pressing is hard work: you bear down on the cap, the screw creaks round as it is driven into the fruit, the plate goes in deeper than it stays and the fruit pushes it back up a little, with a squelch and juice beading out, louder and wetter the further the load is pressed; click the cap for each press (quick clicks are done one after another) or hold it down to keep pressing. Four presses press up to eight apples at once, so nine take eight presses, and nothing is pressed while you stand there without pressing. The mash lies before the fruit, and its juice rains off the plate into a small wooden tub under it within a few seconds, filling it as it runs. Left alone, the press works in Vinery's own time. The press looks the same in the world as at the station, its screw and pressing plate too, and the tub stands under it with its juice in it. Click the tub with empty wine bottles to fill as many as it holds at once (right click: one); with bottles in your inventory an empty hand does it too. The tub holds eight portions: while it is full, the mash waits for you to take them. Honeycomb is pressed the same way, into honey.
- Farm & Charm's mincer is a station: lay what is to be minced on its hopper (carried over it, it stands see-through where it will go, never lost inside the mincer), take the crank by its grip and turn it round with the mouse. The crank is heavy: it lags behind your hand, picks up speed slowly, runs down by itself, and the blade catches on what it cuts four times a turn with a jerk and a crunch; meat goes easily, wood, stone and metal are harder, a full load harder than one item, and the mincer strains and creaks under a hard pull. Up to eight at a time are minced together in two whole turns, so nine take four; what stands in the hopper goes down into it as you turn, and every piece of the load comes out of the die one after another, minced meat pressed out in strands first, and drops onto the mincer's foot, where they lie in a heap until you take them, with crumbs, juice or dust of what is ground. The mincer looks the same in the world, with what stands in its hopper and lies on its foot. Without the mod on the client it is still cranked by right clicks.
- Farm & Charm's crafting bowl is a station: throw the ingredients in, take the bowl's own whisk by its handle (only the handle takes it) and whisk with the mouse, round, to and fro or in figures of eight. What you throw in flies into the bowl, lands with a bounce and lies there as itself, so you see what is in the bowl; liquids are poured from their bucket or bottle, eggs cracked on the rim and flour shaken into it with a puff of dust. A click anywhere on a thing in the bowl takes it back, all of its pieces at once (flour shows where it was shaken in when you point there), and a click anywhere over the bowl puts in what you carry, which is drawn there at its own size. The bowl is seen from higher up than the pot, so that nothing on its floor hides behind its wall. The whisk follows your hand with weight: it lags behind in a thick mixture, leans as it is dragged, wobbles when you stop and knocks on the walls, and it pushes and knocks about what lies in the bowl, which slides, floats and bumps into the walls. As you whisk, the mixture rises and thickens into what it becomes: lumps of it form in the milk or water and run together into a real mass with relief, lit and glossy or matte as the food is. The whisk ploughs furrows through it with ridges on both sides: glossy whipped butter and dough keep their swirls, a porridge keeps the furrows and softens them, and a batter levels again a moment later. A porridge holds its oat flakes and the strawberries cut into it, a salad its leaves, tomato, onion and carrot, all carried round as you whisk and pushed aside by the whisk; pet food turns into grainy mince. The mixture splashes and flicks drops onto the rim, and what lies in it is coated and sinks in. The whole bowlful is mixed at once, and it is work: the mixture holds the whisk back the more it comes together, a thin liquid lets it slip and runs in waves round it, a batter drags at it, damps a jerk and needs steady pushing, and shaking it is no quicker than whisking; one set takes sixteen rounds, a full bowl four times as many. The whisk stays inside the bowl; the thick mixture holds it back only in how it looks and feels, and the bowlful counts your whisking as fast to the end as at the start. Nothing lies where the bowl's front wall hides it from you, and with an empty hand over the bowl what was shaken in, poured or cracked shows faintly where it went in, so a click gives it back (a milk bucket comes back full). What it became shows on the badge beside the bowl; a click there or on the finished bowlful, which lights up under the pointer, takes it; an empty bucket or bottle draws back a liquid poured in by mistake, and what an ingredient leaves (a bucket) comes back to you. The bowl looks the same in the world, with what lies in it.
- At every station the badge beside the block shows what the next step needs from you: the bowl a soup waits for, the bottle for juice, wine or tea, the fuel or heat that is missing (blinking while you have none), and the spoon, the crank, the whisk or the press's cap while the work waits for your hand; while nothing is needed, what is being made. Under it, how much is ready (gold) and how much is still to come.
